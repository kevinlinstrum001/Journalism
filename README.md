# Journalism

A personal research and information system for structured investigation, evidence collection, analysis, modeling, and retrieval.

This repository supports a research workflow designed to investigate questions, preserve evidence, track what is known and unknown, and build durable information resources that can be revisited as new events and questions arise.

The primary consumer of this system is the researcher, with machine-readable structures also maintained so AI systems and other tools can retrieve, compare, and reason across the accumulated material.

## Current Environment

The initial research environment is:

**Las Vegas, New Mexico**

Las Vegas serves as the first working environment in which the research methods, records, schemas, catalogs, viewers, and investigation workflow will be developed and tested.

The repository itself is not limited to Las Vegas, New Mexico. The methods and tools developed here should remain general enough to support other environments and subjects later.

## Purpose

The repository is intended to support:

- question-driven investigations
- documentary and web research
- field observation and field collection
- interviews and direct inquiries
- source preservation and provenance
- entity and relationship tracking
- chronological and event-based research
- comparison of conflicting or incomplete evidence
- structured investigation records
- Markdown research summaries
- machine-readable JSON sidecars
- catalogs and indexes
- research viewers and retrieval tools
- maps, models, datasets, and other analytical products
- documentation of the research system itself

## Operating Model

Research begins with a question, observation, event, information gap, or assignment.

Work is recorded as activities. Activities may include computer-based research, document review, data analysis, field observation, photography, interviews, correspondence, mapping, verification, modeling, or development of the research system itself.

Investigations preserve both the starting and resulting state of knowledge, including:

- open questions
- existing evidence
- possible research approaches
- leads
- sources examined
- findings
- questions resolved
- unresolved questions
- contradictions and uncertainties
- information added to the knowledge system
- further leads and recommended follow-up

## Information Architecture

The project is expected to use several coordinated resources rather than a single authoritative document.

These may include:

- a structured resource and assignment registry
- investigation summaries
- investigation sidecars
- source records
- entity records
- relationship records
- catalogs
- viewers
- datasets
- maps
- field records
- reference documents
- generated reports and other downstream products

Different resources may have different authority for different kinds of information.

## Evidence Principle

Evidence should be preserved with enough provenance to determine what a source actually establishes.

Sources should not be treated as interchangeable. Official records, public communications, datasets, observations, journalism, maps, historical records, interviews, and other materials may each be authoritative for different facts.

Uncertainty, contradiction, incomplete evidence, and unresolved questions should be preserved rather than silently reconciled.

## Repository Status

This repository is in its initial architecture and development stage.

The first working environment is Las Vegas, New Mexico. The registry, investigation method, record formats, catalog structure, and research tools will be refined through actual use rather than fully designed in advance.
