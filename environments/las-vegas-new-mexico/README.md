## 1. Purpose of this README

This README is the local orientation document for the **Las Vegas, New Mexico** research environment.

Its job is to help a person, AI system, or future work session enter this environment and determine:

- what the environment represents;
- where Subjects, Dossiers, Stories, Investigations, Sources, and evidence belong;
- how local records relate to the wider Journalism repository;
- where to look for current research rather than inferring meaning from folder names alone;
- what rules should govern evidence, uncertainty, and cross-domain relationships.

This README is orientation and routing. It is not automatically authoritative for every factual claim about Las Vegas.

> **Use the README to understand the environment; follow references to the record or source that owns the fact you need.**

---

## 2. Environment scope

This environment covers Las Vegas, New Mexico and the systems, institutions, people, places, events, infrastructure, information flows, economics, history, and regional relationships necessary to understand the city and its surrounding context.

Research may include local government, public institutions, business, utilities, water, fire recovery, public finance, construction, environment, media, community information, transportation, geography, historical change, and other subjects that emerge through actual use.

The environment boundary is contextual, not absolute. A federal program, contractor, watershed, state agency, regional institution, or national event may belong in this environment when it materially affects Las Vegas.

---

## 3. The Body

The **Body** is the accumulated substantive material of the Journalism system. It is a logical concept, not a single `body/` directory.

Within this environment, the Body may include:

- source documents;
- field observations;
- images and photographs;
- datasets;
- maps;
- entity and relationship records;
- Dossiers;
- Stories;
- Investigation records and summaries;
- notes, timelines, analyses, and derived artifacts;
- JSON sidecars and catalogs used for retrieval.

A record may support more than one Subject, Story, Dossier, Investigation, or environment. Do not duplicate full records merely to make the folder tree look complete.

---

## 4. Local record families

### Subjects

Subjects provide durable conceptual structure over the Body. They describe areas of interest, organize references, expose gaps, and provide activity state without becoming giant containers for duplicated content.

### Dossiers

Dossiers concentrate material about a particular entity, institution, place, event, program, problem, or other bounded object of continuing interest.

### Stories

Stories are living syntheses. They interpret selected parts of the Body, evolve as events change, and may generate new research requirements.

### Investigations

Investigations are bounded question-driven inquiries. They preserve starting knowledge, research activity, findings, unresolved issues, information added, and follow-up requirements.

### Sources

Sources identify continuing information providers and source artifacts used as evidence, discovery material, context, or objects of analysis.

---

## 5. Evidence and uncertainty

Preserve what a source actually establishes. Do not silently convert allegation into fact, interpretation into evidence, or uncertainty into certainty.

Keep distinct where useful:

- verified fact;
- source claim;
- observation;
- inference;
- commentary;
- provisional interpretation;
- unresolved contradiction;
- unknown.

A news article, government statement, podcast, video, map, dataset, interview, photograph, or field observation may each be useful for different purposes. Authority depends on the question being asked.

---

## 6. Cross-domain relationships

Real-world objects often cross multiple Subjects and systems. A water-treatment project, for example, may simultaneously involve government authority, public finance, contractors, engineering, environmental conditions, public communications, and historical events.

Cross-domain relevance does not require copying an entire record into every category.

> **Repeat identifiers and locally necessary facts; centralize detailed records and analysis.**

## 8. Resuming work

When entering this environment for substantive work:

1. Read this README.
2. Determine the Subject, Story, Dossier, Investigation, Source, or Assignment involved.
3. Check the Registry for current routing and work state when available.
4. Read the nearest relevant local README or current record.
5. Identify the sources and authority appropriate to the question.
6. Preserve new evidence and uncertainty in the Body rather than only in conversation.

The environment should become easier to resume as the Registry and catalogs mature.
